==================================================
5. WHY THE OLD FRONTEND POD STILL SERVES TRAFFIC
   WHILE THE REPLACEMENT POD IS FAILING
==================================================

1. A RollingUpdate keeps old Pods until new Pods are Ready.
   The Deployment uses RollingUpdate with maxSurge=25% and
   maxUnavailable=25%. With replicas=1:
     - maxSurge 25% of 1 rounds UP to 1, so 1 extra Pod may be created.
     - maxUnavailable 25% of 1 rounds DOWN to 0, so 0 Pods may be
       unavailable.
   The controller therefore creates the new Pod first (surge). It only
   removes the old Pod after the new one is Ready and Available. The
   new Pod never becomes Ready, so the old ReplicaSet (5f584df999) is
   never scaled down. This is why 2 frontend Pods exist.

2. Services only route to Ready endpoints.
   Both Pods carry the label app=book-review-frontend, so both appear in
   the EndpointSlice. However, the new Pod's endpoint is marked
   ready=false / serving=false, and kube-proxy routes Service traffic
   only to ready endpoints. All requests go to 10.244.0.91 (the old Pod
   on :assignment10).

3. The Deployment still meets its availability target.
   Because 1 replica is Available, the Deployment reports
   Available=True. Progress has stopped (ProgressDeadlineExceeded),
   but Kubernetes does NOT roll back automatically. It leaves the old
   Pod running and the new Pod retrying.

Impact:
  - Users are currently served the previous version (:assignment10).
  - The intended new version is NOT deployed.
  - There is a single point of failure. If the old Pod is deleted or
    evicted, or its node fails, the replacement would be created from
    the broken revision-5 template. The frontend would then go fully
    down, with 0 ready backends.
  - The failing Pod keeps generating image-pull and probe-failure noise.
